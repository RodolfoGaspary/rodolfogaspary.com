The problem

Most small businesses in Lima approach a developer without knowing what they are buying: they do not distinguish between a site that exists and a site that appears when someone searches for what they sell. The site had to do two things at once — attract enquiries, and demonstrate by being the example itself what the difference is.

How I solved it

Static HTML, no framework and no CMS. That is not an aesthetic position: a five-page site does not need a build layer, and removing it eliminates maintenance, security updates and the load time a CMS drags along. The copy is written in Peruvian informal Spanish, with prices visible and the process laid out in four steps, because the real client objection is not technical — it is uncertainty about cost and timeline.

The decision that defines the project

I optimised the site not only for Google but for generative engines. When someone asks an AI assistant for a developer in Lima, the answer is assembled from content the model can read and cite: schema.org structured data, areaServed covering the regions I serve, and pages that answer concrete questions instead of describing services in the abstract. It is a bet on how people will look for suppliers over the next few years.

How it is built

  • Hand-written HTML and CSS, no framework and no build step
  • Schema.org structured data, including geographic service coverage
  • Open Graph tags, canonical URLs and indexing control
  • Blog articles aimed at the questions clients actually search for
  • Direct WhatsApp contact, the channel the local market genuinely uses
